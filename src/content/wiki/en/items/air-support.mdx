## What air support is confirmed?

The Steam page names bombers and gatling gun helicopters as air support options. It describes them as a response for when zombies break through the perimeter and the spread is in danger of getting out of control. The presskit also mentions air strikes and helicopter support.

The official pages do not publish damage, blast radius, cooldown, cost, or targeting rules. This guide avoids those invented details. What can be stated confidently is the strategic role: air support is a fast response layer above ground defenses, and it carries a civilian-risk warning.

![City under heavy infection with air and ground response markers](/images/game/screenshot-1.webp)

## When is air support justified?

Use it when a ground response cannot arrive in time and the breach threatens a populated area or the integrity of the whole defense. The store page explicitly warns players to aim carefully to minimize civilian casualties. That warning should be part of the decision, not an afterthought.

A bomber and a gatling gun helicopter may feel different in use, but both should be evaluated by the same questions: What is the immediate threat? What else is inside the affected area? Can ground forces solve it with less risk?

## How should you choose a target area?

Look for the point where the infection is crossing from a contained problem into an uncontrolled one. A dense cluster near a breach may be a better target than a scattered group far from civilians. The official material does not define an ideal target shape, so observation matters more than a fixed rule.

Check the surroundings before committing. Roads, buildings, and civilian concentrations affect the consequences of a strike. A target that stops the breach but creates a new gap in the protective line may not improve the overall situation.

## Why should ground defenses remain primary?

Air support is described as a response to a breakthrough, which implies that the main plan should still be ground defense and preparation. If every crisis is answered from the air, the underlying layout may be too weak or too spread out. Use aviation to buy time for a repair, not to replace the repair.

The [ground forces guide](/items/ground-forces/) covers the units that hold the line. The [defenses guide](/items/defenses/) covers the structures that shape movement. Read both before deciding that more air support is the answer to repeated breaches.

## How do you reduce civilian risk?

Make civilian protection the first filter. The [civilian protection guide](/safe-zones/) explains why the score depends on survivors and how safe zones and evacuation routes change the trade-off. Air support should be called when the alternative is worse, not simply when a target looks convenient.

After a strike, observe whether the breach actually closes and whether civilians remain in danger. If the same area fails again, the next fix should address the layout or the ground response. Repeating the same strike is not a strategy.

## What should you test in a retry?

In a retry, compare a run that preserves air support for emergencies with one that uses it earlier. Keep the rest of the plan as similar as possible so the comparison means something. Check civilian losses as well as whether the perimeter held.

The game encourages retrying and adjusting strategy, and the official description explicitly says losing is part of the process. Start with the [beginner guide](/beginner-guide/) if you are still learning the prepare-then-outbreak loop, then use air support as a deliberate emergency tool rather than a reflex.
