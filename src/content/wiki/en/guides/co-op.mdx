## How many players can join?

The Steam feature list says you can play with one other friend in online co-op multiplayer. It does not describe public matchmaking, dedicated servers, or a larger party size. Plan for a two-player session unless the store page changes.

Two players can cover more of a city, but they also create a coordination problem. If both players reinforce the same breach, another district may be left alone. If neither takes responsibility for civilians, the military plan can succeed while the score suffers. Agree on a simple division before the outbreak begins.

![Large city view showing multiple districts and spread of infection](/images/game/screenshot-4.webp)

## What roles should each player take?

A practical split is one player on the main perimeter and one on civilian protection and emergency response. The perimeter player watches the first point of contact and repositions ground forces. The support player watches protected areas, civilian routes, and whether air support is needed. Swap roles between runs so both players learn the whole system.

These are suggestions, not a tested competitive meta. The official page does not publish co-op-specific balance details. What matters is that each player knows which decisions belong to them and which require a quick check with the other.

The [ground forces overview](/items/ground-forces/) and [air support overview](/items/air-support/) can provide shared vocabulary before a session. For the objective itself, read the [civilian protection guide](/guides/civilian-protection/).

## How should you communicate during a run?

Use short, specific updates: which area is failing, whether civilians are exposed, and what support is already committed. Avoid narrating every zombie group. The other player needs actionable information, not a continuous commentary.

When a breach occurs, state the response before spending it. "I am moving the garrison" is more useful than "I will handle it." If one player calls air support, the other should know the target area so they do not duplicate the response or move units into it unnecessarily.

## Does single-player pacing carry over?

Do not assume that single-player pause or speed behavior works the same way in co-op. The official store description does not explain how time control is synchronized. Confirm the actual behavior in your session and agree on a convention before relying on it.

If either player can change pace, sudden changes can disrupt the other's plan. Otherwise, both need to stay ready for continuous pressure.

## What should you do after a failed run?

Restart together rather than silently rebuilding alone. Name the main failure: an uncovered approach, a delayed evacuation, an unsafe air strike, or a plan that was too spread out. Then choose one change for the next attempt.

The game explicitly encourages retrying and adjusting strategy. Co-op makes that easier because two players can compare observations, but only if they share them. A short review after each loss is more valuable than immediately launching another run with the same layout.

## Which maps suit co-op?

Start with a developer-made city so both players can learn the common rules. Move to Workshop maps after you understand the basic division of labor. A custom map may change density, routes, or civilian pressure, so read its description and check that it is intended for two players.

The [maps and Workshop guide](/guides/maps-and-workshop/) covers how to evaluate listings. If either player has older hardware, check the [system requirements](/guides/system-requirements/) and lower settings before blaming the network. The [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) is the place to start if either player is new to the prepare-then-outbreak loop.
