## Why do civilians matter more than kills?

The mission is won by eliminating all zombies, but the score is determined by how many civilians you saved. That distinction matters. A defense that clears streets quickly while abandoning residential districts can still produce a poor result. Protection is not a side objective; it is the measure of how well the plan worked.

Start by identifying where people are concentrated and which routes connect them to safer areas. The official feature list names safe zones, gates, barricades, and evacuation stations as protective tools. Their exact costs and coverage are not published in the store description, so place them according to the city's geometry rather than assuming a universal layout.

## Where should safe zones and gates go?

Think in layers. An outer layer slows or redirects the horde, a middle layer gives civilians time to move, and an inner layer protects the area you cannot afford to lose. Gates and barricades can shape movement, but a barrier with no nearby response force may simply delay a breach. Pair protective structures with units that can cover the approach.

Avoid isolated islands of safety. A safe zone surrounded by open streets may be difficult to reinforce once the infection spreads around it. Prefer connected protected areas with more than one route for civilians and defenders. If the city layout forces a narrow chokepoint, make sure a failure there does not trap the population behind it.

The [defenses guide](/items/defenses/) explains the general roles of protective structures. For the units that support them, see [ground forces](/items/ground-forces/).

## How do you choose between containment and rescue?

Containment tries to stop the spread at a distance. Rescue focuses on keeping civilians alive inside an area that is already under pressure. Both are useful, but they compete for limited resources. A common beginner mistake is to overbuild the outer perimeter while leaving the interior without a fallback.

When resources are tight, protect the population center first and extend outward only where the extension supports that goal. A shorter, better-supported line is easier to understand than a long line with gaps. This is a planning suggestion, not a tested optimum; city layout and outbreak behavior can change the right answer.

## When should air support be used near civilians?

Air support is an emergency answer, not a safe default. The Steam page specifically warns that strikes must be aimed well to minimize civilian casualties. Before calling a bomber or gatling gun helicopter, check what is inside the target area and whether ground forces could solve the problem with less risk.

If a breach is already spreading into a populated district, a delayed response may be worse than a carefully placed one. The practical rule is to use the least destructive tool that can stop the immediate threat. Read [air support](/items/air-support/) for the trade-offs between firepower and collateral risk.

## What should you review after a run?

After the mission, compare civilian losses with the places where the defense broke. Did people die because there was no protected route, because a gate failed, or because a response arrived too late? The answer should change the next layout, not just the number of units.

The official design encourages retrying and adjusting your strategy. Treat each retry as a controlled experiment: change one protective assumption, then observe whether civilians have more time and safer paths. The [beginner guide](/beginner-guide/) and [maps and workshop guide](/guides/maps-and-workshop/) can help you frame those experiments.
